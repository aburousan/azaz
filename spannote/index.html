New in version 0.9.

What is new in this version.

fresh ink ↘
1.

A mask for anything.

The eraser has a new Mask mode. Paint over writing, shapes or pictures and they are hidden, while the paper and its lines stay. Draw it freehand, or as a line, box or circle, filled or as an outline. Choose what it hides, for example only handwriting. Undo brings it all back.

2.

Planner pages.

Daily, weekly and monthly pages, a to-do list, a habit tracker, meals, a timetable and a journal. Pick them when you make a notebook or change a page's paper.

3.

A Starry night theme.

A night sky with swirls, stars and a small village behind your library and your pages.

4.

Settings that follow you.

If you sync two tablets, your settings, pens and toolbar now go across too. So does each notebook's cover.

5.

Sticky notes and a calculator.

Both now sit on the toolbar. Hide them or move them in Settings › Tools › Toolbar. A sticky note carries its writing when you move it.

6.

A tidier library.

Hold a notebook to start picking several at once. Each card shows how big the notebook is.

7.

Snapping your way.

Moving or turning a selection lines it up with what is nearby. You can now turn this off, in the lasso's menu or in Settings.

8.

Smoother on modest tablets.

Writing is lighter on tablets with less memory or a 60 Hz screen. Saving a very big notebook is much faster.

9.

Fixes.

Ink that sometimes vanished after writing is fixed. So are undo after a mask and Reset crop on a picture whose background was removed.

Things to try in the app.

A few small exercises for your first evening with SpanNote.

pen ready?
a.

Work out a derivative.

  1. Write d/dx(x³ sin x) with the pen.
  2. Draw round it with the lasso.
  3. Tap Work out. The answer, 3x² sin x + x³ cos x, is written beside it.
b.

Draw a perfect circle.

  1. Draw a rough circle and keep the pen down at the end.
  2. Wait half a second. It becomes a clean ellipse.
  3. Keep holding for a perfect circle.
c.

Hide a working.

  1. Tap the eraser again to open its menu and pick Mask.
  2. Choose the box shape and draw over a part of your notes.
  3. Tap undo to bring it back.
d.

Plan your week.

  1. Make a new notebook.
  2. Under Planners, pick the weekly page.
  3. Write your week straight onto it.
e.

Neat handwriting.

  1. Write a short line of words.
  2. Tap Pretty at the top.
  3. Your words come back in neat handwriting, each one where you wrote it.
f.

Fill with colour.

  1. Draw a closed shape, a star or a house.
  2. Drag a colour from the toolbar into it.
  3. The colour fills just that part.
g.

A graph on the page.

  1. Tap Insert, then Graph.
  2. Change its curve to sin(x)/x.
  3. Drag and pinch inside the graph to look around.
h.

Cut out a photo.

  1. Add a picture of a person or a pet.
  2. Hold your finger on it and pick Remove background.
  3. Changed your mind? Reset crop brings the whole photo back.
i.

Turn a molecule.

  1. Open the stickers and find 3D molecules.
  2. Put benzene or DNA on your page.
  3. Drag its 3D handle to turn it.
01

Start with a good pen.

Six tools, each with its own way of leaving a mark.

some ink to play with ↘

Fountain pen

Pressure opens the line. Optional sheen adds a second colour where the ink pools.

Ballpoint

A simple, even line for everyday writing.

Brush pen

Pressure and speed shape the stroke. Adjust the bleed to soften the edge.

Pencil

Soft graphite on the paper grain. Press harder for a darker line, go over it again to build it up, lean the pen to shade.

Highlighter

Round or chisel tip. Crossing strokes of one colour keep the same shade. Lean it for a soft wide shade.

Technical pen

Wavy, zigzag and double lines, or a little picture repeated along the line. Bring your own picture as an SVG.

Pressure and tilt work when your pen supports them. You can also write with an ordinary stylus or your finger.

Made for maths and physics.

Things a notes app usually sends you to another program for. Here they happen on the page, and all of it works offline.

no second app needed ↘
1.

Calculus, exactly.

Write d/dx(x³ sin x) or ∫ x eˣ dx, lasso it, and the exact answer is written beside it. Definite integrals give a number.

2.

The functions physics uses.

Gamma, Beta, zeta, erf, Bessel J and I, Legendre, Hermite, Laguerre and Chebyshev polynomials, elliptic integrals, Lambert W, Si and Ei. Ready to use in sums and graphs.

3.

Solve it.

Equations such as x² − 5x + 6 = 0 or sin x = 0.5 are solved, and roots are found to about 12 digits.

4.

Matrices by hand.

Write a matrix with brackets and it is read from its rows and columns. Determinant, inverse and eigenvalues, complex ones too.

5.

Graphs inside your notes.

y = f(x), implicit curves, parametric curves. Shade the area under a curve and its integral is written in. Fit a line, quadratic, cubic or exponential to your points, with R².

6.

Surfaces you can turn.

z = f(x, y) and parametric surfaces: sphere, torus, Möbius strip, Klein bottle, helicoid, even a Calabi–Yau slice. Add a vector field, a gradient, a divergence or a curl in 3D. Turn them with a finger, right on the page.

7.

Typeset maths.

LaTeX in text boxes and in graph labels. Written maths can become typed maths (experimental).

8.

Diagrams that look right.

Wavy lines for photons and springs, zigzag and double lines, arrows that stay attached to their shapes. Draw a Feynman diagram or a free-body diagram in a minute.

9.

Measure on the page.

A ruler and a protractor that lie on the page: read lengths and angles, draw straight along the edge, zoom to real size.

06

Work out the maths, on the page.

For the small calculations and the longer ones.

20! is rather large.

Lasso it. Tap “Work out”.

Select handwritten maths and the answer is written beside it. For example, 20! = 2432902008176640000.

Functions, calculus and matrices.

Use the Maths panel for derivatives, integrals and matrices. Find roots of equations to about 12 digits. Place typed maths wherever you need it.

A few worked examplesExamples of app output

∫ x ex dx = (x − 1)ex

ln(e²) = 2

3D

Science you can turn.

Stickers for physics, chemistry and biology: laws and reactions typeset, molecules in ball-and-stick, and real anatomy.

Drag the 3D handle to turn them.

Skeleton, skull, spine, rib cage, brain, heart, lungs and kidneys from BodyParts3D (© DBCLS, CC BY 4.0), and molecules from water to DNA. Turning them is a change of view, so it never fills your undo.

Famous laws and reactions.

Maxwell, Schrödinger, Einstein and Dirac; Haber, Contact and Ostwald processes; photosynthesis, respiration and Michaelis–Menten. Tap one and it is on your page, ready to edit.

13

The rest of the notebook.

Small things that make a difference over a long day.

a few more notes...
a.

Two pages of thought.

Open two notebooks side by side in split screen. Keep the reference beside the rough work.

b.

Snip and link.

Take a snip from your notes. Link related bits so you can find your way back.

c.

Find your handwriting.

Search what you wrote by hand. Handwriting is read on the tablet and never sent anywhere.

d.

A copy somewhere safe.

Back up to a folder, Nextcloud/WebDAV or Google Drive. Keep tablets the same through Google Drive, WebDAV or a private GitHub repo. Save .sp, PDF and audio, with a folder for each notebook. Passwords are kept encrypted on the tablet.

e.

A change of light.

Dark, Light, Cosmology, Anime and Starry night themes. Invert the pages when writing at night.

f.

Room for a sticker.

54 emoji from Noto Emoji (Apache 2.0), and science stickers: famous physics laws, chemical reactions and biology equations, typeset so they print sharp. SpanNote works on any Android device running version 10 or newer, with or without pen pressure and tilt.

g.

A cover of your own.

Twelve colours, twelve of the author's own drawings, or any photo. Write on the cover too, or leave it off.

h.

Turn the page your way.

Scroll down through the pages, or swipe sideways one page at a time, like a book.

i.

Write into a text box.

Write with the pen in and around a text box and it is typed in for you.

k.

Molecules and bodies in 3D.

Water, methane, benzene, salt, DNA and more as ball-and-stick models, and a skeleton, skull, spine, brain, heart, lungs and kidneys (from BodyParts3D, CC BY 4.0). Drag the 3D handle to turn them on the page.

l.

A maths reader of its own.

SpanNote reads handwritten maths on the tablet, with physics symbols: vectors, bras and kets, daggers, dots, Greek capitals, integrals to infinity. Fix a reading in LaTeX and it learns what you meant (only if you choose to send it).

m.

Solids you can size.

Cubes, cones, cylinders, spheres, prisms and pyramids that turn in 3D with their axes shown. Set the radius and height in centimetres.

j.

Tidy writing (experimental).

The wand turns your written words into neat handwriting of the same size and colour, still ink you can erase. Typeset turns written maths into typed maths. Both are still learning.
